How it works

Follow one payment from the checkout to your wallet.

1

The customer picks a network

You share a link or open a session from your server. The customer lands on the hosted checkout and presses Generate payment.

2

They send the exact amount

A connected wallet signs once and we pay the network fee. Any other wallet or exchange can send to the address shown, with 30 minutes on the clock by default.

3

A short payment shows what is left

If an exchange deducts a withdrawal fee, the checkout says how much is missing and the customer tops up the same address before the timer ends.

4

The network confirms it

A payment counts after 10 blocks on Base. The checkout shows the progress, so nobody has to refresh or guess.

5

Your server is told and your wallet is paid

The payment counts as Paid and we send a signed webhook so you can fulfil the order. One transaction then pays your wallet and the fee.

Account security

Locked by default. Unlocked when you are.

Changing where your money goes takes a code from your email or your authenticator app. Unlock once, make your changes, and your account locks itself again.

  1. Locked

    A code before anything changes

    A new wallet, a new API key or a new webhook endpoint in Live mode asks for a 6-digit code first. A stolen password alone changes nothing.

  2. Unlocked 10:00

    One code for a run of changes

    Setting up? One code unlocks this device for 10 minutes: add wallets, create keys and endpoints, and watch the time left in the corner.

  3. Locked again

    It locks itself

    After 10 minutes, when you sign out, or the moment you press Lock now. Using it never makes it last longer.

  • Every new wallet is emailed to youUnlocked or not, you hear about each one.
  • Key rolls always ask againRolling an API key or a webhook secret takes its own code.
  • Only the device you unlockedAnother browser or device still asks for its own code.
Questions

Before you connect a wallet.

Do you ever hold my money?

No. Each payment goes to a deposit address that can only pay the wallets you set and the fee. There is no Prismaport balance to withdraw from.

Which networks and tokens can I accept?

USDC on Base today. Solana is coming later.

What if a customer sends the wrong amount?

A short payment shows Partially paid and the checkout says what is left to send to the same address. If more arrives than the amount, the session shows Overpaid and the excess is yours.

What if a payment arrives after the timer ends?

By default, a payment up to 23 hours and 30 minutes late still reaches you. The session shows Late payment and you get a webhook, so you can decide whether to fulfil the order.

Who pays the network fee?

We pay the network fee to move funds to your wallet. We also pay it when a customer signs with a connected wallet. A customer who sends the transaction from their own wallet pays that transaction's network fee.

How do refunds work?

Refunds come from you, the merchant, because Prismaport never holds your payment.
